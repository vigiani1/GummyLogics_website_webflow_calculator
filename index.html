Recipe Guide & Instructions

Complete step-by-step methodology

Temperature is the whole game. Never exceed 180°F. If you see steam rising, you are too hot and you are burning off terpenes and cannabinoids.

The Simplified Method

Infused gummies stand out for potency and purity, but building them from scratch is time-consuming and technical to formulate correctly. This method starts from pre-made gummies and infuses them with liquid lecithin, MCT oil, and your THC extract of choice — rosin, distillate, RSO, isolate, or any other extract — so you skip gelatin blooming, preservative math, and complex formulation entirely.

By starting with professionally manufactured gummies you inherit their texture, flavor balance, and shelf stability — and you get unlimited flavor options from whatever bulk candy you can source.

Step-by-Step

1

Prepare your workstation

  • Set up the double boiler on the hotplate for indirect heat
  • Use a precision scale for gummies and extract
  • Have an oven-safe glass jar (Pyrex works well) on hand for melting the extract mixture separately
2

Prepare the extract mixture

  • In the glass jar, warm your measured THC extract over low, indirect heat until fully melted into a liquid, stirring often to keep it from scorching
  • Stir in the lecithin until fully and evenly incorporated
  • Mix in the MCT oil, then set the jar aside — this is your extract mixture
3

Melt the gummies

  • Heat gently, stirring continuously
  • Never exceed 180°F — steam means too hot
  • Continue until completely melted into a smooth liquid slurry
  • Optional: a little water compensates for moisture loss, but softens the final texture
4

Combine and dose

  • Once the gummy base is fully melted into a liquid slurry, slowly stream in your prepared extract mixture
  • Add your mold-inhibitor drops (potassium sorbate, 1 drop per 250 g of total mixture)
  • Add any optional citric acid, food coloring, and flavoring, then stir until fully combined
5

Homogenize

  • A hand mixer works well, or use an overhead mixer for hands-free consistency
  • 15–20 minutes is typically enough
  • Thorough mixing is imperative — an under-mixed batch creates hot spots, leaving some gummies too strong and others too weak
6

Prepare and fill molds

  • Lightly coat the molds with demolding oil or cooking spray
  • Pour the slurry into the middle of each mold, staying away from the edges to avoid spilling over
  • Use a heated scraper to spread the slurry into every cavity, working quickly so it does not solidify before you finish spreading
7

Set the gummies

  • Let the molds sit undisturbed until fully solid
  • Refrigerating speeds this up; cover with plastic wrap to prevent surface drying
  • Set overnight, 8–12 hours minimum, for the most consistent texture
8

Demold, cure, and store

  • Demold carefully once fully set, coating in sanding sugar or carnauba wax to finish
  • Cure in a stable room and log the temperature, humidity, and duration
  • Airtight container, refrigerated

Using the Three Solve Modes

Target gummy count — the classic forward calculation. You want 100 gummies; it tells you what to buy and weigh.

Limited by gummy base — you have 454 g of Albanese left in the bag. It back-solves the largest whole-gummy batch that fits and tells you the leftover.

Limited by extract on hand — you have 5 g of extract and that is all you have. It back-solves how many gummies you can dose at your target potency.

In both limited modes the count is floored to a whole gummy, then every other material is recomputed from that whole number, so the recipe you get is always actually mixable.

Reading the Cure & Waste Log

Unit waste compares finished count to planned count. This is your headline number — broken gummies, mold overfill, pieces that stuck.

Mass loss in cure compares finished weight to total batch mass. During a cure most of this is water leaving the product, which is normal and often desirable for texture.

Water content splits that finished weight into water and dry solids. Two batches can weigh the same and be very different products — this is the number that tells you which.

Log temperature, humidity, and duration every run. After a handful of batches you will see which conditions gave you the lowest unit waste, and you can set your overage from data instead of guessing.

Extract Buffer & Overage

Leave the extract buffer at 0. It exists for one situation only: you sent a batch to a lab, the result came back under your target dose, and you want the next batch to compensate. Without a lab number there is nothing to base a figure on, so guessing here only makes your dose less predictable, not more. At 0 the tool doses your extract at face value using the THC percentage on your test results or your own notes.

If you do have a lab number, the math is simple: divide your target dose by what the lab actually measured. A batch aimed at 25 mg that tested at 22 mg means you retained 88%, so a 12% buffer brings the next run on target.

Overage works on count instead of potency. Set 5% and it plans a 105-gummy batch so that after normal scrape and fill loss you still walk away with 100 sellable gummies.

Gummy Selection

Albanese is the go-to: 5 lb bags sorted by single flavor, no picking through mixed assortments, cost-effective for consistent production. Pineapple + mango pairs beautifully with fruity extract flavors; red cherry is a bold standalone.

Albanese uses corn syrup as the primary ingredient. If cleaner labels matter, look at German Haribo for superior gelatin quality or Melt To Make, which is formulated specifically for infusion.

Equipment: The Overhead Mixer

Homogenization separates amateur from professional. Without it you get hot spots — pockets of high cannabinoid concentration that make dosing unpredictable across the same batch.

The LabFish overhead stirrer gives precision RPM control in the critical 100–200 range and lets the mixture homogenize uninterrupted for 5–10 minutes, which is effectively impossible by hand.
